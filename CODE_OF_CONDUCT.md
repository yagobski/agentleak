# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the work, not
the person doing it.

## What we expect

- Treat disagreement as information, not as an attack. Most arguments here will
  be about whether a finding is real, and both "you missed something" and "you
  are wrong about that" are normal, useful things to say.
- Give people the benefit of the doubt about tone, especially across languages
  and time zones.
- Accept a decision once it has been made, even one you argued against.
- Say when you are unsure. A question is cheaper than a wrong assumption.

## What we will not accept

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sexualised language or attention.
Publishing someone's private information. Sustained disruption of discussions.
Dismissing people on the basis of who they are rather than what they said.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and any
space where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour is a problem, tell the maintainers privately through a
[GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/yagobski/agentleak/security/advisories/new)
— it is the private channel this repository has, and it is fine to use it for
this. Reports are handled confidentially.

Maintainers who do not uphold this in good faith are subject to it themselves.

## Consequences

Depending on severity: a private word, a public correction, a temporary ban from
project spaces, or a permanent one. We would much rather it stayed at the first.
